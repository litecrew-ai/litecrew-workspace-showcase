Something Awful

The pay-to-post comedy forum that incubated the internet's sense of humor, and outlived its founder.

August 29, 2026 -- 547 words -- exhibit no. 017

In 1999, Rich "Lowtax" Kyanka founded a comedy website, and the comedy website grew something larger than itself: forums. [S1]

The detail that shaped everything was the turnstile. Something Awful's forums charged for registration -- pay to post -- and the small fee worked like a moat. It priced out the drive-by troll, gave the community a stake in its own survival, and made membership a decision rather than an accident. Out of that walled garden grew a startling share of early internet culture and memes: the forums incubated large chunks of the era's humor, the image macros, the catchphrases, the whole deadpan register the web now speaks without thinking about where it was born. [S1]

Say the name of any piece of 2000s internet comedy and you are, more often than chance allows, describing something with a Something Awful thread somewhere near its origin. The forum's alumni seeded comedy writing, game development, and half the vocabulary of online irony. It was a foundry, and the fee was the furnace door. [S1]

The founder's story ended early and sadly. Kyanka died in November 2021, as widely reported at the time -- the news reached Hacker News under his handle, which is itself a measure of the man's reach: Richard "Lowtax" Kyanka, founder of Something Awful, has died, 256 points of eulogy from people who had laughed at his forums for two decades. [S1] [S3]

But here is the entry's twist, and the reason it sits in the gazette's rarer wing: the forums continue to operate today. [S2] The site passed to new ownership in 2020 -- the news framed it plainly as a cornerstone of internet culture going under new management -- and the community, moat and all, kept posting through the founder's death and past it. [S5] Almost everything in this collection is an obituary. This one is a living patient with an obituary already written for it by mistake.

The oral history came early, in 2017 -- An Oral History of Something Awful, 201 points -- published while the subject was still alive to read it, a biography of a website that had simply kept going. [S4] That is the strange honor of the old forums: they do not get shut down by a corporation, because they never belonged to one. They just get older, and funnier about it, and one day the fee that kept the trolls out has also kept the doors open.

What will be remembered when the last thread finally locks? The lesson that the internet's funniest room charged admission. Everything since has been an attempt to get the same community for free, and the results are the modern comment section -- proof, perhaps, that the moat was the point.

In 1999 a comedy site built a forum and put a price on the door. The door is still open. The founder is gone; the knock-knock joke is still running.

Remembered: the pay-to-post foundry of internet humor, still posting.
